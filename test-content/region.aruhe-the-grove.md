# The Grove

Central grove of [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]. The island's last room. The [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] is bound here. The Death Bloom is graves, tree, and ritual ground together — not an object that can be pocketed. It must remain satisfying if the party reaches it without ever fighting the Blight: the climax here is the choice to **destroy, preserve, or bargain over the Death Bloom**. The broader island route topology is deferred; this page owns the Grove's final-room logic.

[[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]] is hyper-nature gone sick. This clearing is the same appetite held still. Grass too green. Fruit that was ripe when whoever set it died, and is ripe now. Sweet rot that never finishes. The fauna at the tree line do not hunt. They watch.

![[assets/places/aruhe-the-grove.jpeg]]

## Overview

> [!narration] Overview
> [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]]'s luminous roots end in a clearing too green for the air around it. A fruit tree stands over two unmarked graves. Black flowers ring the grass. No root crosses either grave. Through the tree line, a still lake holds the sky. Fauna gather at the boundary and watch without moving. The air is sweet with rot that will not finish. Stop when they step to the graves, touch the fruit, or turn back into [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]].

> [!tip] Arrival
> Do not introduce the Blight or roll initiative when the party arrives. Stop narration and give the players room: this is the emotional answer, not a combat trigger. The Grove only responds when something takes, the tree is struck, or a grave is dug up.

## Thesis

Everywhere else on [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]], nature has been prevented from stopping: growth becomes excess, hunger, mutation, and rot. Here nature has been prevented from continuing. The Grove is grief made biologically permanent, a dead person's room preserved exactly as they left it. Nothing may be moved, because changing anything feels like losing them again.

> Governing rule: **Look. Remember. Do not touch. Nothing leaves. Nothing changes.**

Central question: **Is grief allowed to own an island forever?**

## Geography

Innermost band. One small clearing, roughly **100–150 ft across** — a clearing-sized memorial, not another vast biome. At the tree line runs the **Root Verge**: every luminous Rot root converges toward the Grove, then stops abruptly. Sap-lines from [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]] arrive and halt. The tree, two unmarked graves, and a ring of black flowers occupy the whole usable floor. No root crosses either grave. The rest of the island's growth is excess. Here the excess is composure — a garden that will not go on or go back.

The ground is the [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]]'s own body. The grove knows who enters. There is no second door. Out is back through [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]]. Climbing down to [[location.aruhe-the-clear-lake|The Clear Lake]] is leaving the last room, not another way in. No cave route reaches the Grove either — [[region.aruhe-the-cave|The Cave]] is alternate routes and dangerous shortcuts, not a second door; the Grove remains the last room. The island-scale Expedition Turn and route-state procedure remain deferred to [[dungeon.aruhe-wilderness-megadungeon|Aruhe Wilderness Mega-Dungeon]].

The land is still working, and the work is memorial. Fruit hangs and is not taken. Flowers stay open. The mourner line does not break unless something takes, or the tree is struck.

The final-room contrast is absolute. Outside, growth and predation overrun their jobs. Inside, ripe fruit never changes, flowers stay open, graves reject roots and decay, predators stop hunting, and every luminous root halts at the memorial boundary. This is not ordinary quiet. It is [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]'s overactivity made biologically still.

## Approaches and boundary

- **Rotward approach.** The party can follow the luminous root-roads from [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]]. They point here and stop at the Root Verge. The route is fast and legible, but the roots report movement toward the Grove. Avoiding them is slower and quieter. [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]] owns the route's travel costs.
- **Lakeward approach.** [[location.aruhe-the-clear-lake|The Clear Lake]] remains visible below the lakeward shelf. The shore and its outlet streams explain the approach, but the water is not a second door: climbing down leaves the final room, and no swim or flooded route enters the Grove. The graves remain on land above the freshwater bowl.
- **Mourner boundary.** The black-flower ring and tree line are the legible threshold. [[creature.terror-bird|Terror-Birds]], [[creature.corpsewood|Corpsewood]], [[creature.aruhe-crown-squid|Crown Squids]], [[creature.deer-stalker|Deer-Stalkers]], and other established fauna gather there without hunting, grazing, or crossing. Visitors may enter. The mourners do not, unless the memorial is violated.

## Climate

Still air. Sweet rot that never finishes. Sound dies at the tree line the way it dies in [[region.aruhe-the-quiet|The Quiet]], then stays dead. No spore-exhaustion from [[region.aruhe-the-rot|The Rot]] once the grass begins — the air here is wrong in the other direction: too clean, too sweet.

The Grove holds almost no conventional hazards: no random encounters, and Rot exhaustion ends at the boundary. Characters may rest, investigate, argue, sketch, cast divinations, or simply sit — observation does not trigger hostility.

## Peoples

None live here. [[faction.grung-clans|Grung Clans]] will not land on [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]] at all. The nearest settlement is still [[region.karath|Karath]], weeks away across the interior and a half-mile channel. Castaways leave or stay from the beach, not this grove.

## Threats

> [!danger] Active Threats
> [[creature.terror-bird|Terror-Birds]] stalk the last approach. [[creature.corpsewood|Corpsewood]] walk the perimeter and stop at the mourner line. The ground is the Blight. The tree's fruit hangs as the last temptation. Destroying the Death Bloom ends the [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] permanently. Leaving it preserves the sanctuary and its killings.

The only dangers here are the ones the party brings: damaging the Deathbloom directly attacks the Blight, and digging up either grave is **extreme desecration**. Arrival does not automatically require combat. The Grove is quieter than most of [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]].

- `[[creature.blight]]` — place-bound. Stage 1 while the tree is whole, Stage 2 when wounded, Stage 3 near ruin. Body HP is not the gate. Real damage on the Bloom drops a stage: [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] → [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] → [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]].
- `[[creature.terror-bird]]` — Grove / Rot apex on the approach. Ground shakes before the charge.
- `[[creature.corpsewood]]` — walking deadwood on the perimeter. Does not cross the flower ring unless the tree is struck.
- `[[creature.aruhe-crown-squid]]` — does not cross the mourner line; part of the standing fauna that watches at the tree line.

**The fruit.** Same healing temptation as the terraces, on the memorial tree. The Bloom's fruit is deliberately **not numerically superior** to the island's other miracle fruit — perfectly ripe, untouched, hanging over the graves.

**Black-flower ring.** Ornament at the mourner line. Visitors may enter. The mourners do not, unless the memorial is violated.

## Death Bloom states

The Death Bloom is the tree, both graves, the black-flower ring, the bound soil, and the ritual relationship between them. It is a place-bound memorial, not a detachable magic object. Its state changes only when the party causes meaningful damage to that living and ritual fabric.

> [!danger] The objective is the Bloom
> [[creature.blight|Hinewai]]'s body HP is not the objective or the state track. Attacking her body can buy time or defend the memorial, but it does not move the Death Bloom between states. Real damage to the Bloom changes the Grove and swaps the established staged Blight link. If her body is destroyed while the Bloom remains intact, she can reform at the memorial.

| Death Bloom state | Grove | [[creature.blight\|Hinewai]] and staged Blight link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole** | Fruit, flowers, graves, roots, and the mourner line hold their perfect stillness. The Grove responds as a complete memorial. | Composed and strongest. Use [[creature.blight\|Blight Stage 1]]. |
| **Wounded** | The first damage makes the memorial visibly unstable. Flowers and roots can react, but the graves remain held and the boundary stays legible. | Wounded and furious. Use [[creature.blight\|Blight Stage 2]]. |
| **Failing** | The Grove's defenses lose coherence. Stasis begins to fracture, and the root network can no longer answer every intrusion cleanly. | Desperate and near ruin. Use [[creature.blight\|Blight Stage 3]]. |
| **Destroyed** | The memorial binding is severed. The Grove does not explode. It begins a slow ecological release, and the old perfect stillness can no longer return. | [[creature.blight\|Hinewai]] cannot reform from the Death Bloom. The staged body is no longer the objective. |

The state transition is a player choice, not a required combat sequence. Bargaining, preserving, returning what was taken, or leaving the Grove intact all keep the Bloom Whole. Striking [[creature.blight|Hinewai]] does not substitute for damaging the memorial.

## Landmarks

- **Deathbloom Tree** — an otherwise ordinary fruit tree, its species tied personally to the companion; it reproduces the fruit he loved, echoing the origin story in which the island presented it to her during the burial. Fruit hangs at perpetual perfect ripeness — no immature fruit, no blossom cycle, no seasonal change, no senescence — and removed fruit begins aging normally.
- **Death Bloom** — the tree, both graves, the black-flower ring, the bound soil, and the ritual relationship between them. A place-bound phylactery/memorial, not a removable magic object: destroying the tree still drives the Blight's stage progression and ultimately ends her, but it should never read as "a magic phylactery tree."
- **First Grave** — the older grave, belonging to the drowned companion. **Second Grave** — the later grave, the Blight's own, tied to the island-wide root network. Both unmarked, beneath the tree; no root crosses either one. The gap is the fact.
- **Black-flower ring** — around the grass. Exchange markers if anyone knows to look. Boundary the mourners will not cross.
- **Mourner line** — fauna standing still at the tree line. They watch. They do not graze.
- **Lakeward shelf** — the clearing hangs above one shore of [[location.aruhe-the-clear-lake|The Clear Lake]]. The lake remains visible through the trees, reinforcing where the original loss occurred. The graves stay on land.

> [!tip] Magical readings
> **Detect Magic:** necromancy, transmutation, and abjuration permeate the entire area. **Identify:** the tree is magically altered living tissue, not a magic item. **Commune with Nature:** the Grove is the organizing source of [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]'s corruption. **Speak with Plants:** the tree remembers impressions rather than exposition — *She carried him here. She stayed. She spoke. She went beneath. We keep them.*

> [!tip] Reading the graves
> **DC 12 Investigation:** both depressions are deliberate graves. **DC 14:** the graves were dug years apart. **DC 16:** the second grave appears intentionally self-prepared — nobody remained afterward to maintain it. **DC 13 Nature/Medicine:** decomposition should make buried soil biologically active. **DC 15 Nature/Medicine:** the contradiction is deliberate — roots, fungi, insects, and seedlings are excluded from the graves while the surrounding land is hyper-fertile.
>
> The Blight did not merely preserve memory. She **refused to allow Nature to recycle her companion's body**. The companion's grave is the **reason**; the Blight's grave is the **mechanism**.

> [!tip] The mourners
> [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]'s major predators behave impossibly peacefully at the boundary: [[creature.terror-bird|Terror-Birds]], [[creature.corpsewood|Corpsewood]], [[creature.aruhe-crown-squid|Crown Squids]], and [[creature.deer-stalker|Deer-Stalkers]] — or any other established fauna. [[creature.aruhe-crown-squid|Crown Squids]] canonically refuse to cross the line. Inside the Grove, predator does not attack prey, scavenger does not eat, territorial fauna do not posture, animals do not graze, nothing competes — every ecological rule the party has learned stops at once. Do not run them as initiative pieces waiting for a trigger; they are **mourners** unless the memorial is violated.

> [!secret]- Secrets
> The Blight began as an archdruid's refuge-protecting ritual. Corruption made her its physical form. Her companion drowned in the ocean channel during their escape from the [[creature.grung|Grung]]; she carried him inland and buried him beneath this tree, then made the second grave her own. Reach the Bloom without taking and she meets the party calmer (Stage 1, no preemptive fauna). She knows they came either way. [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] owns the personal name and surviving memory. Destroying the tree ends her. Leaving it leaves the island hungry.

> [!secret]- If the Blight never appears
> The Grove must tell her entire story on its own. Let the party infer, in order: (1) the whole island points here; (2) monsters refuse to enter; (3) two people are buried beneath the tree; (4) one died years before the other; (5) the second person deliberately joined the first; (6) nature is being actively prevented from consuming either grave; (7) the entire island exists to preserve this memorial. Only afterward should the Blight's appearance or conversation become optional additional context.

> [!secret]- If the Bloom falls — or stands
> If destroyed, no explosive magical collapse. The first signs are small and biological: wind finally crosses the clearing, a black flower closes, fallen fruit finishes rotting, an insect lands on it, leaves begin behaving seasonally.
>
> | Horizon after destruction | Ecological release |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Weeks** | Root coordination stops and the broader corruption begins to recede. Fruit and flowers no longer obey perfect stasis. |
> | **Months** | Territories shift. Populations migrate, compete, and settle into new patterns as the island loses its old regulator. |
> | **Years** | Populations crash, normalize, or leave. Some species may disappear, [[region.aruhe-the-river\|The River]] changes, [[region.aruhe-the-quiet\|The Quiet]] slowly acquires sound, and fruit finally overripens and falls. |
>
> If preserved, return visits look almost identical regardless of elapsed years: same ripe fruit, same open flowers, same grass, same graves, same roots, same waiting predators. The memorial holds stasis.
