## Overview

> [!narration] Player-facing description — read or adapt at the table.
> Something fat and pale is working its way into the throat of the flower.
>
> It is only a caterpillar. A large one — longer than your thumb, with a waxy cream-green body and little black feet clutching the petals. Its mouth works steadily at the blossom.
>
> When the branch moves, it stops chewing.
>
> The grub pulls itself tight. Two dark lines appear beneath its translucent skin, one yellow, one almost clear. They rush toward one another. Its body begins to swell.
>
> There is a noise like fat hissing in a pan.
>
> **Pop.**
>
> Fire fills the space where your hand was. When your ears stop ringing, there is no caterpillar on the flower. There are little scorched things wriggling through the leaves twenty feet away. Several of them.

A hand-sized caterpillar whose defense is detonation. [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]'s familiar-thing-doing-the-wrong-job theme carried to its logical end: it never grew enormous because **nothing can safely eat it**. The explosion is not a suicide tactic — it is reproduction.

## Ecology

**Bombardier chemistry.** The Grubnade keeps two long abdominal reservoirs: one peroxide/hydroquinone-rich, one enzyme-rich catalytic. A muscular valve normally isolates them. Stress hormones open both into a heavily reinforced blast chamber. This is real bombardier-beetle chemistry (real discharge ≈ 100 °C), but the five-foot fireball is explicitly an [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]] exaggeration: the [[creature.blight|Druid Lich]] added volatile plant oils to the discharge, the reaction atomizes them into a hot aerosol, and that flashes into the brief fireball.

**Permanently paedogenetic.** There is no butterfly and no moth — there may be no adult stage anymore. A mature Grubnade carries **1d4 + 1 partially developed daughters** in chambers down its abdomen (real insects reproduce before adulthood; some gall midge larvae develop inside other larvae).

**The explosion is reproduction.** Each internal daughter grows around one of the parent's segmental regenerative nodes. By the time a Grubnade can detonate, its body is pre-perforated into viable animals. When it bursts, the disposable central blast chamber ruptures while pressure blows the daughter-segments outward through predetermined seams, each wrapped in clear heat-resistant mucus, thrown 10–30 feet. A few seconds later they uncurl and crawl away. Predation is the trigger for asexual reproduction; the corpse *is* the clutch. ^[inferred from design: source frames the blast-birth as intended take-the-premise-literally]

**Why the island leaves it alone.** Most small birds and insects in [[region.aruhe-the-quiet|The Quiet]] have vanished beneath the island's grotesquely enlarged food web. Grubnades are among the exceptions: predators capable of learning have learned not to bite the fat pale caterpillars, and predators incapable of learning select themselves out. Semicircular feeding marks on leaves persist because nothing eats the eater of them. ^[inferred]

## Behavior

**Harmless until touched.** A Grubnade has no attack and no offensive behavior. It ignores anything that stays more than a few inches away. Normal footsteps do not trigger it — the party must interact with the apparently harmless thing.

It becomes **disturbed** if:

- a creature touches or picks it up;
- it takes any damage;
- a creature deliberately shakes or crushes the plant supporting it;
- its space is subjected to significant fire, thunder, or bludgeoning damage.

**Thermal runaway.** When disturbed it contracts violently, its valves rupture, and the reservoirs empty into the blast chamber. For roughly half a second its body visibly inflates, the skin turns translucent, the two fluids race together, and it produces a rising fizz/hiss while the plant beneath it curls from heat. It detonates **at the end of the current turn** — that delay is the whole encounter. The emotional register is a lithium battery going into thermal runaway, not a grenade: *it's swelling, it's getting hot, put it down.* ^[inferred: source names this as the intended fear reference]

**Hot bloom.** Grubnades normally spread out because each needs a lot of foliage, but enormous flowering plants sometimes attract several at once. A flower holding **three or more mature Grubnades** is a **hot bloom**; if one detonates, any other mature Grubnade in its blast takes fire damage and is disturbed, so the battery-propagation fear is real but rare.

**Predator deference.** Learned predators refuse the caterpillar: a [[creature.deer-stalker|Deer-Stalker]] steps around one; a [[creature.wolfrabbit|Wolfrabbit]] that smells a mature Grubnade in foliage aborts its pounce; [[creature.terror-bird|Terror-Birds]] avoid flowers containing them; even a [[creature.aruhe-river-otter|Aruhe River Otter]] presented with one will sniff, recoil, and leave it alone. A creature that attacks everything else suddenly refusing to touch a caterpillar is the campaign's best foreshadowing that something is off.

## Habitat

- **[[region.aruhe-the-old-gardens|The Old Gardens]]** — primary habitat; the absurd flowers, citrus, yams, and rampant plant growth are exactly the food supply they want.
- **[[region.aruhe-the-quiet|The Quiet]]** — the revelation habitat: the jungle is famously missing its small-animal soundscape, and yet you find neat caterpillar bites in giant leaves, little blackened circles on flowers, occasional scorched patches five feet across, and predators with old burn scars around the muzzle.
- **[[region.aruhe-the-grasslands|The Grasslands]]** — occasional, especially inside large flowering plants along [[region.aruhe-the-river|The River]] cuts. Never in huge numbers; the charm is being an easily overlooked piece of [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]], not another biome-defining threat.

## Lore

> [!secret]- What is actually known about this creature — collapsed by default.
> Sailors named them Grubnades. The name is the grenade joke; the fear underneath is the mundane object that is harmless until physical damage starts an accelerating chemical reaction inside something you're holding. A veteran explorer does not look frightened at one Grubnade. They look frightened at **three caterpillars feeding from the same flower**.

## Tactics

> [!tip] Running this creature — see the linked statblock for the runnable loop.
Runnable loop: [[creature.grubnade]]. It is a **living trap**, not a combat monster — the players have to interact with the harmless thing. First encounter: make it almost cute, fat and absorbed in eating; the warning is that it does not try to crawl away. Things that can run from predators run. This one does not need to.

**Spotting** (checks reveal what the caterpillar *means*, never hide the basic creature): DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) to notice one among dense foliage before pushing through; DC 13 Intelligence (Nature) to read the translucent reservoirs as defensive organs; DC 15 Intelligence (Nature) to identify the brood chambers and see it is carrying several developing copies of itself. After seeing one explode, no check is required ever again — [[region.aruhe|Aruhe]] works better when players learn rules than when characters roll to remember them.

A character can use an action to pluck and throw a known Grubnade as an improvised weapon (normal 20 ft., long 60 ft.); picking it up disturbs it, and it explodes on impact or at the end of the thrower's turn. It works.

## Variants

**Juvenile** — Tiny, translucent, roughly finger-joint sized, hurled 10–30 feet by the blast. Juveniles **cannot detonate**; after seven days of feeding, each becomes a mature Grubnade. This is the critical brake: one Grubnade produces four unarmed Grubnades, never an immediate combat cascade.

**Mature** — the runnable hazard above.

## Relationships

- **[[creature.blight|Druid Lich]]** — the corruption that made the bomb: volatile plant oils in the discharge, regenerative capacity fused with paedogenesis.
- **[[creature.deer-stalker|Deer-Stalker]], [[creature.wolfrabbit|Wolfrabbit]], [[creature.terror-bird|Terror-Bird]], [[creature.aruhe-river-otter|Aruhe River Otter]]** — predators that refuse to touch it; old burn scars around their muzzles tell the history.
- **[[region.aruhe|Aruhe]]** — its theme in miniature: a familiar thing doing the wrong job, and one of the few insects the island cannot eat.
