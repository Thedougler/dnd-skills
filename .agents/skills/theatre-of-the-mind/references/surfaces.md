# Player-facing surfaces

Theatre of the mind owns every player-facing prose surface. The camera,
staging card, pointable anchors, fold, and located change remain the method.
Choose the output branch before you draft.

| Surface | Form | Branch rule |
|---|---|---|
| Live narration | Spoken block in `[!narration]` titled **Narration** | Present tense, third person, one camera, stop at the reaction point. |
| NPC speech | Separate `[!narration]` titled **Narration — <who speaks>** | Quote the line. Give the table one question, demand, offer, or answer. Leave room to interrupt. |
| Recap | `[!narration]` on a session guide or report | Use past tense. State what play made true. End on tonight's live handle. |
| Combat update | Spoken block or short live line | Name the acting body, its position, the change, and the next threat or opening. Keep routine hits short. |
| Handout or rendered player text | Player-safe renderer output from its diegetic owner | Keep only facts the player may receive. Preserve the owner's voice and pointable details. Never include `[!secret]-` material. |

## Vault wrappers

The spoken text is the answer. The callout title is the storage surface, not a
line for the DM to read aloud. Use `Narration` for description, recap, combat,
and a narrated transition. Use `Narration — <who speaks>` for quoted speech.

Put skills, DCs, saving throws, and other procedure in a separate
`[!mechanic]` callout. The narration gives the fiction that makes the check
meaningful. The DM speaks it, then calls the roll. Do not hide a DC or a
mechanical instruction inside the spoken block.

Keep DM-only truth in a collapsed `[!secret]-` callout. A handout or renderer
may show a secret only after the table has earned that reveal. Its source page
still owns the truth.

## Recap

A recap has a different job from a first look. Use past tense. Include only
what the last session made true and what is already in motion tonight. The
final sentence is the live handle that carries into the first current scene.
Do not replay every event or dictate what the party thinks about it.

## Dialogue

Give the speaker one beat at a time. The first prepared line should ask,
press, offer, answer, or threaten. Keep a thank-you, aside, and warning as
separate beats when the table should be able to interrupt. Scenery and DM notes
stay outside the quote.

## Handouts and rendered text

A handout is player-facing because the player can read it without the DM's
filter. Apply the same boundary as speech. Remove hidden facts, unsourced
certainty, private DM interpretation, and future outcomes. Keep the handout's
diegetic owner, one clear purpose, and the facts a player can point to, quote,
or act on. Do not duplicate a canonical owner when a renderer can link or
transclude it.

## Branch finish

Before delivery, ask which surface you wrote. A narration listener must know
where they are, what they see, what is moving, and what matters now. A recap
listener must know what changed and why tonight starts here. A dialogue line
must leave an answerable opening. A handout must expose no hidden truth.

Every branch must still pass the first-hearing sketch test and end before the
player's next meaningful choice.
