# Special branches

Read only the heading that applies. The complication deepens the
Signature.

## Consumable

Consumables can be bolder because their power is finite. Give the
player a meaningful moment to choose *when* the effect is worth
spending.

A good consumable has a clear tempting effect, an obvious quantity or
use limit, enough specificity to inspire creative timing, and no
maintenance after it is spent.

A consumable so precious or narrow that rational players never use it
has failed.

## Curse — Temptation → Tell → Cost → Release

**Temptation:** The item offers a real benefit or fulfills a desire.
The curse is interesting because using the item is attractive.

**Tell:** Seed discoverable signs — material wrongness, history,
behavior, dreams, reactions, contradictory provenance.

**Cost:** The curse changes choices, relationships, risk, or tactics.
Prefer consequences the player can play around or knowingly risk.

**Escalation:** If the curse grows, connect growth to use, violated
taboos, the item's purpose, or narrative events. Keep the rule
observable enough that the DM can run it.

**Release:** State how the curse can be broken, suppressed,
transferred, satisfied, or transformed. Standard cursed-item rules
make most identification methods fail to reveal the curse and prevent
voluntary ending of attunement until the curse is broken; custom
exceptions must be explicit.

If loss of agency is part of horror, make its scope, signals, and
avenues of resistance worthy of the table's tone.

For commerce, distinguish apparent value from informed value; a
reputable buyer who discovers a curse may refuse the item entirely.

## Sentient — Voice → Want → Boundary → Bargain

Treat a sentient item as a person attached to an object. Sentience
stays on the item. Mint `npc` only when the spirit is independently
addressable as a person.

Define:

- **Voice:** personality, verbal style, emotional texture
- **Want:** special purpose and immediate desire
- **Boundary:** taboo, fear, refusal, prejudice, or condition for cooperation
- **Bargain:** what good relations unlock and what strained relations threaten
- mental ability scores and alignment when the rules need them
- communication mode and senses
- what the item actually knows, including gaps, lies, and mistaken beliefs

The DM controls the sentient item and its activated properties.
Relationship can govern cooperation and access. The item creates
conversation.

A sentient item may offer clues or perspective. It holds the knowledge
a being of its origin would have. Preserve the distinction between the
item's beliefs and world truth.

## Evolving — Promise → Milestone → Deepening

Use staged items when the reward should remain emotionally important
across tiers. One page holds every stage.

- Establish the core promise in the first state.
- Unlock later states through meaningful character or world milestones.
- Re-benchmark every state against the tier in which it will be used.
- Let each state deepen the original Signature: wider application,
  stronger consequence, a new mode, or a revealed truth.
- Prefer two or three memorable stages.
- Make the awakening itself fiction: repaired seal, fulfilled oath,
  reconciled spirit, recovered name, reforging, sacrifice, discovery,
  or major feat.

A progression track is prepared possibility until its trigger becomes
true. If an awakening must be tracked in campaign time, represent that
occurrence as an `event`.

## Artifact

Artifacts are unique, priceless, and usually campaign objects before
they are equipment.

Give an artifact a world-scale origin and purpose; powers worthy of
its narrative role; costs, enemies, claims, duties, or consequences
equal to its importance; a special destruction condition if
destruction matters; and a reason the campaign cannot treat it as
ordinary merchandise.

If a low-level character encounters an artifact-scale concept, use
dormant, sealed, or incomplete power.

## Item set

Each piece should be satisfying alone. A set bonus should create a new
combination, interaction, or story revelation.

Use sets to express shared provenance — regalia, relics, broken
armament, planar instruments.

Each piece is its own `item` page. State the set bonus on each piece
and point at the other members.
