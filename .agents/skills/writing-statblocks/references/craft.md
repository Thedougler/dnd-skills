# Statblock craft

Numbers, signature tests, and usage fit. The operational skill lives in `../SKILL.md`.

## Numbers

Benchmark against the closest current rules-compatible statblocks for:

* AC and defensive features
* HP
* attack bonus and save DC
* expected damage per round
* number of attacks
* mobility
* saving throws
* resistances and immunities

Spread expected damage across multiple attacks at higher CR rather than concentrating the whole budget into one hit. Price reliable bonus-action, reaction, aura, ongoing, summon, and off-turn damage into total output. Multi-target effects should deal substantially less per target than the creature's full single-target round.

Prefer HP, movement, positioning, limited reactive defenses, or visible mitigation when durability is needed. Extreme AC and repeated immunity create empty turns.

## Signature

A strong signature ability does at least one of these:

* changes positioning
* changes who wants to target whom
* creates or removes terrain
* establishes a temporary objective
* changes the value of a resource
* rewards or punishes a recognizable behavior
* creates a transformation or escalation
* reveals something important about the creature

## Use

### Adversary

Keep the default turn obvious. Add complexity only where it changes tactics.

Its abilities should cooperate with its role, terrain, and plausible allies without requiring a predetermined encounter composition.

### Solo

A solo must contest a party's action economy.

Give it, as appropriate:

* multi-target pressure
* meaningful movement
* off-turn Legendary Actions or reactions
* a way to recover from or play through encounter-ending control
* an escalation, transformation, or changing tactical state
* reasons to affect more than the nearest PC

Off-turn options should move the fight, alter positioning, deploy the creature's fantasy, or change priorities.

A staged boss should tell a story through play: establish → escalate → desperation.

### Ally

Optimize for **spotlight support**.

Give an ally:

* one immediately recognizable specialty
* a tiny set of meaningful choices
* minimal bookkeeping
* abilities that create opportunities for PCs rather than replace their specialties

A DM or player should be able to operate it without managing another PC character sheet.

### Narrative force

Some statblocks exist principally to define **how the party can engage with an extraordinary being**, not to promise a fair damage race.

Express the fiction through bounded mechanics such as:

* protection or sanctuary
* taboos and triggered consequences
* bargains or permissions
* escape and pursuit
* environmental control
* defensive superiority
* restorative or transformative powers
* explicit vulnerabilities, leverage, or conditions that change the relationship

Make the intended affordances discoverable. Combat may be possible while negotiation, protection, appeasement, escape, rescue, theft, or proving worth remains mechanically meaningful.

If there is no runnable 5e timing — when, who, how, what, how long, how it ends, how often — it is not a statblock.

## Three-round check

When stress-testing, look at:

* expected three-round damage
* concentration of damage on one target
* off-turn output
* control duration and escape opportunities
* mobility and range
* action-economy pressure
* recharge / limited-use impact
* defensive stall potential
* ally spotlight cost, if applicable
* whether intelligent tactics match its actual Intelligence, training, and goals
